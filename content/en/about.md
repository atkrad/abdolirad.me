I'm Mohammad Abdolirad, a software and platform engineer based in the Netherlands.

I've been building software professionally since 2013. My work has taken me through backend development, infrastructure, automation, and cloud-native technologies.

These days, I primarily work with Go, Kubernetes, and the surrounding cloud-native ecosystem.

I'm particularly interested in developer experience. I enjoy building tools that make developers' lives easier, whether that's simplifying deployments, designing APIs, or making complex systems more approachable.

I also believe good engineering involves knowing when *not* to introduce more complexity.

GNU/Linux has been an important part of my development environment for years. I currently use NixOS and enjoy exploring Nix, reproducible development environments, and ways to make development workflows more consistent and reliable.

### Open Source

Open source has been an important part of my work for years. I enjoy building tools, experimenting with ideas, and sharing what I've learned.

Some of the projects I've worked on include:

- **[Wait4X](https://wait4x.dev/)**: A command-line tool for waiting on services and dependencies to become available.
- **[Rivaas](https://rivaas.dev/)**: A cloud-native web framework for Go.
- **[Deployah](https://deployah.dev/)**: A tool designed to simplify application deployments to Kubernetes.
- **[Nabat](https://github.com/nabat-dev/nabat)**: A CLI framework for Go, focused on developer experience.
- **[Gopherly](https://gopherly.dev/)**: A collection of Go packages focused on developer experience, clarity, and composability.

Each project has given me opportunities to explore different approaches to software design, usability, and maintainability.

### About This Blog

I use this blog to write about software engineering, technical decisions, and things I've learned from building and maintaining software.

I'm interested in more than documenting how something works. I want to explore why certain decisions were made, what alternatives existed, and what I might do differently next time.

Some articles will be technical. Others will be more personal reflections on engineering, learning, and the occasional mistake.
